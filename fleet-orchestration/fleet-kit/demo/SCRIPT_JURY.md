# Jury pitch: 2:30, English

SUB-15 Fleet Orchestration. Slot **2:30** (hard window 2:00 to 3:00), with a **2:00 cut** below. Jury: army officers,
investors, potential military customers, mostly **not technical**. **One presenter**, who also drives the laptop. Demo
scenario: **L4 seed 3** (100 drones: 15 scouts, 50 light and 35 heavy strikers; 90 contacts), every page built from the
tag `handin-final` (`c33b1ef`).

The pitch is built around **link loss and safety**, our strong point. Other teams are ahead of us on total score, so
no score ratio is said on stage: the ratios stay in the Q&A, behind an honest first answer.

**Plain words on stage.** No kit jargon in the spoken lines or on the slides: no "S_loss", "retention", "seeds",
"bytes", "B/s", "validator", "R4" or "R8". The exact figures sit in the speaker notes ("If asked"), in the Q&A table and
in "Facts behind the lines", which says where each claim comes from.

Story:

1. **Hook:** the hard part is not the drone, it is a hundred of them as one team, safe when the radio dies.
2. **Problem:** once the radio is cut, each drone is on its own; one mistake and the mission counts for nothing.
3. **Live demo:** a no-fly zone appears after the cut; the attack group stops together.
4. **Proof:** 8,000 test runs on the organisers' own grid, zero failures.
5. **On-board rules:** the five rules every drone follows on its own.
6. **Safety first:** the better-scoring version we threw away.
7. **The mission plan:** a safe plan always ready, every refusal explained.
8. **Close.**

Two browser tabs only: **Cmd+1** the deck (presentation mode), **Cmd+2** `view_linkloss.html`. Advance the deck with
the right arrow. Space plays and pauses the viewer.

## Q&A reserve

Answer in plain words first; the figures in brackets are there if someone presses. The score questions come first: be
ready for them.

| Likely question | Answer (1–2 sentences) |
|---|---|
| Your total score is behind other teams. Why pick you? | Some teams score more points, and we say so. We put our effort where a front line needs it: no failure when the radio dies, no plan ever rejected, a safe plan always ready, and every decision explained; where we had to choose, we chose safety. |
| How do you compare with the standard plan? | Over 200 missions we never tuned on, we get 1.6 times the mission value of the organisers' standard plan, 2.2 times on the hardest 100-drone missions, and never less on any mission. (Final set seeds 9000–9049: 99,580 against 62,029. Demo mission: 39 targets handled against 18.) |
| Why is yours better than the other teams'? | We can't see their plans. What we can show: zero failures with the radio cut on the organisers' grid, zero rejected plans, never worse than the standard plan, and an explanation for every decision. Ask every team for those four. |
| Where does it still lose points? | One known case: on one test mission we keep only 61 % of the value, still with no failure. Two drones pull out just after take-off and wait above the base, in the way of the drones leaving; sending them home fixed it but broke three other missions, so we left it out. (Scenario L3-6, retention 0.611.) |
| Why so many messages? Could the drones talk less? | We tried a quieter version. It scored about 2 % higher, but it failed once, and one failure can cost a mission, so we kept the safer one. (About 9 messages per drone per minute; score 0.967 against 0.949 at 1000 m × 200 B/s; 1 failed run at 250 m, sizing score 0.75 against 1.00.) |
| How do the drones learn about the zone? | The drones close enough to see it, two of them here, spot it the second it appears and tell the others. The first drone heading into it stops; its teammates stop one second later, when its message arrives. Drones whose path stays clear carry on. (H02 and L22 within 300 m; L02 at 639 s, H02, L10, L27 at 640 s.) |
| Why stop instead of flying around the zone? | The challenge rules give a drone no way to invent a new route once the radio is cut: it can follow its plan, wait, slow down or turn back. Stopping is the only safe answer, and it costs one attack, not the mission. |
| What if a target turns friendly? | The drones heading to it stop well before reaching it, every time. Hitting a friend fails the mission, so this rule is never relaxed. (They stop 30 s before the target.) |
| Is it fast enough for real hardware? | Each drone takes a fraction of a millisecond to decide; the organisers ask for well under 50 ms and mention a small Raspberry Pi board for finalists. No special hardware. (0.05 to 0.08 ms per decision, Python standard library.) |
| What if the planning computer crashes or runs out of time? | A safe plan is saved before it starts thinking; if it runs out of time or crashes, the best safe plan found so far goes out automatically, and it keeps the standard plan whenever that one is better. We tested each fault by causing it on purpose. (Search stops at 38 s, a 52 s alarm writes the plan, limit 60 s.) |
| Who uses it, and when? | The drone unit's mission planner, before launch: fleet, targets and no-fly zones in; a safe plan and a one-page brief out in under a minute. If the radio drops in flight, the rules on each drone take over. |
| What would it take to field it? | Real sensor data with its uncertainty, real radios, the unit's own data formats, and a legal review of the engagement rules. None of that is in the challenge, so we don't claim it. |
| Why leave a valuable target alone? | Three kinds of reasons, each shown to the operator: a rule (this one is 140 m from a civilian, the limit is 200 m), not enough drones free (one target needs four light strikers and all fifty are busy), or out of reach in its time window. |
| Why not machine learning? | Learned solvers need a graphics card, and they don't handle attacks that must arrive together or the 40 m spacing; on a laptop in under a minute, a search over explicit rules did better. It also lets us explain every decision by a rule, which a neural network can't. |
| What happens at 500 drones? | The planning time per step shrinks with fleet size to stay under a minute, and the safety net always hands in a valid plan. An earlier version was valid up to 500; we haven't re-timed the final version at that size. (38 s search at 100 drones, 22 s at 500.) |

If asked about margins: the **Alerts** tab of `view_ops.html` ranks the tightest ones. Top line: L07 and L34 come
within 41 m at H+00:06:48 (limit 40 m); the tightest confirmation is C04, struck 10.3 s after its observation (minimum

## If the live demo fails

1. **Server down or browser stuck:** open `view_linkloss.html` straight from the demo folder (double-click). The page
   is self-contained and makes no network calls. Reload a stuck tab with Cmd+R: it restarts at t = 0, and the Link tab's
   event buttons jump straight to the right moment.
2. **The `popup_zone` button does nothing:** drag the timeline to the second orange diamond (t ≈ 618 s; the red
   dashed line before it is the cut at 434 s), press Space, pause at t ≈ 645 s.
3. **Viewer fails during part 3:** stay on the deck, slide 3 is the same moment as a still; say the same lines.
4. **Laptop, browser, projector or Wi-Fi fails:** present from the exported PDF (laptop or phone); its speaker notes
   carry these lines.
5. **Any number disputed:** point to the "Facts behind the lines" table and offer the repo.

## How it was timed

- Words counted per part and converted at 140 words per minute (a slower human pace). The earlier versions were also
  read by macOS `say`; this one was counted on Windows, so it has no `say` measure.
- 2:30 version: **308 words, 2:12** at 140 wpm. Add about 14 s for the slide changes, two tab switches, the
  `popup_zone` click and the four-second playback (partly spoken over): **about 2:26**.
- 2:00 cut: **243 words, 1:44** at 140 wpm, **about 1:58** with clicks.
- Playback speed (measured earlier in the browser): 8× moves 16 s of mission per 2 s, so t = 615 to 645 s takes about
  4 s.
- Still to do: one rehearsal out loud, clicking for real, with a stopwatch. People presenting in a second language
  often speak slower than 140 wpm; if the run goes over 2:30, use the 2:00 cut.
