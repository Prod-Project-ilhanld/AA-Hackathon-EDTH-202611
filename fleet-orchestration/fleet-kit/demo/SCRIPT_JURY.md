# Jury pitch: 2:30, English

SUB-15 Fleet Orchestration. Slot **2:30** (hard window 2:00 to 3:00), with a **2:00 cut** below. Jury: army officers and
investors. **One presenter**, who also drives the laptop. Demo scenario: **L4 seed 3** (100 drones: 15 scouts, 50 light
and 35 heavy strikers; 90 contacts), every page built from the tag `handin-final` (`c33b1ef`).

The pitch is built around **link loss and safety**, our strong point. Other teams are ahead of us on total score, so
no score ratio is said on stage: the ratios stay in the Q&A, behind an honest first answer. Every spoken claim is
measured; "Facts behind the lines" says where each one comes from.

Story:

1. **Hook:** the hard part is not the drone, it is a hundred of them as one team, safe when the link dies.
2. **Problem:** what the kit's link cut really means, and that one violation scores zero.
3. **Live demo:** the link-loss replay, a pop-up zone, the strike group holds.
4. **Proof:** the official sizing grid.
5. **On-board rules:** the five rules every drone flies with.
6. **Safety first:** the variant we threw away.
7. **Planner safety nets:** the same idea before take-off, and every refusal explained.
8. **Close.**

Two browser tabs only: **Cmd+1** the deck (presentation mode), **Cmd+2** `view_linkloss.html`. Advance the deck with

## Q&A reserve

The score questions come first: be ready for them.

| Likely question | Answer (1–2 sentences) |
|---|---|
| Your total score is behind other teams. Why pick you? | Some teams score more points, and we say so. We put our effort where a front line needs it: zero failed runs when the link drops, zero invalid plans, a fallback that always hands in a valid plan, and every decision explained; where we had to choose, we chose safety. |
| How do you compare with the simple plan? | On 200 runs on seeds we never tuned on (final set 9000–9049): 1.6 times its score overall, 2.2 times on the 100-drone level, and never below it on any seed. On the demo scenario: 39 contacts served against 18. |
| Why is yours better than the other teams'? | We can't see their plans. What we can show: zero failed link-loss runs on the official grid, zero invalid plans, never below the baseline, and an explanation for every decision. Ask every team for those four. |
| Where does the link-loss policy fail? | One known case: on scenario L3-6, retention is 0.611, because two drones abort just after take-off and hover over the base, head-on with departing drones. Flying them home fixed it but broke three other scenarios, so we didn't ship that. |
| Why so many messages? Could you cut traffic? | We tried a lower-traffic variant: about 9 messages per drone per minute and 0.967 against 0.949 on paper, but one failed run at 250 m and the sizing score fell to 0.75. A failed run scores zero, so we kept the safer policy. |
| How do the drones learn about the zone? | Two drones within 300 m, H02 and L22, sense it the second it appears and broadcast it; drones whose route stays clear carry on. L02 holds at 639 s before its route enters the zone, and the rest of its strike group holds one second later, on its message. |
| Why hold instead of flying around the zone? | The kit gives a drone no way to invent a route: it can only follow its plan, hold, slow down or turn back. Holding is the only safe answer to a new zone on the route, and it costs the strike, not the run. |
| What if a target turns friendly? | Strikers due on it keep to their plan, which is already deconflicted, and stop 30 s before reaching it. Engaging a reclassified target is a failed run, so this rule is never relaxed. |
| Is it fast enough for real hardware? | One policy step takes about 0.05 to 0.08 ms on our machines; the kit asks for well under 50 ms and mentions a Raspberry Pi 5 for finalists. Standard library only. |
| What if the planner crashes or runs out of time? | A plan file is written before the search starts, the search stops at 38 s, a 52 s alarm writes the best valid plan found, any error falls back to the simple plan, and we keep the simple plan whenever it scores better. Each fault is tested by injecting it. |
| Who uses it, and when? | The drone unit's mission planner, before launch: fleet, contacts and zones in; a valid plan and a one-page brief out in under a minute. If the link drops in flight, the policy on each drone takes over. |
| What would it take to field it? | Real sensor data with its uncertainty, real radios, the unit's own data formats, and a legal review of the engagement rules. None of that is in the challenge kit, so we don't claim it. |
| Why leave an 85-point target? | Three reasons, each on a Why card: a rule (C27 is 140 m from a civilian), resources (C50 needs 4 light strikers and all 50 are in use), or reach (C37: no route reaches it in its time window). |
| Why not machine learning? | Learned solvers need torch and a GPU, and they don't handle synchronised strikes or the 40 m separation; in 60 s on a laptop, a search over explicit rules did better. It also lets us explain every decision by rule, which a network can't. |
| What happens at 500 drones? | The search budget shrinks with fleet size (38 s at 100, 22 s at 500) to keep the run under 60 s, and the safety net always writes a valid plan. An earlier version was valid up to 500; we haven't re-timed the final version at that size. |

If asked about margins: the **Alerts** tab of `view_ops.html` ranks the tightest ones. Top line: L07 and L34 come
within 41 m at H+00:06:48 (limit 40 m); the tightest confirmation is C04, struck 10.3 s after its observation (minimum
10 s).

## If the live demo fails

1. **Server down or browser stuck:** open `view_linkloss.html` straight from the demo folder (double-click). The page
   is self-contained and makes no network calls. Reload a stuck tab with Cmd+R: it restarts at t = 0, and the Link tab's
   event buttons jump straight to the right moment.
2. **The `popup_zone` button does nothing:** drag the timeline to the second orange diamond (t ≈ 618 s; the red
   dashed line before it is the cut at 434 s), press Space, pause at t ≈ 645 s.
3. **Viewer fails during part 3:** stay on the deck, slide 3 is the same moment as a still; say the same lines.
4. **Laptop, browser, projector or Wi-Fi fails:** present from the exported PDF (laptop or phone); its speaker notes
   carry these lines.
5. **Any number disputed:** point to the "Facts behind the lines" table and offer the repo.
