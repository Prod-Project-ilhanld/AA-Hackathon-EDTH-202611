The problem

No commander can hand-plan a 100-drone strike in real time

100

drones to coordinate: scouts that confirm, strikers that engage

90

contacts on the map, with civilians and friendly forces close by

40 m

minimum distance between any two drones, at all times

20 s

window for the drones of one strike to arrive together

No-fly zones to avoid, and every strike must be confirmed by a scout first.

Source: the organisers’ challenge rules; full-scale test mission (100 drones, 90 contacts).

“A commander has a hundred drones, scouts and strikers, dozens of targets, civilians nearby, no-fly zones. Every strike confirmed first, drones forty metres apart, strikes within twenty seconds of each other. Nobody can plan that by hand in real time, and one mistake means a wrong target or a collision.” If asked: the rules are the challenge’s 10 rules of engagement (R1 to R10): confirmation by a scout at least 10 s before the strike (R4), 100 m protection around friendlies and civilians (R6), no strike within 200 m of them (R7), 40 m separation (R8), time windows (R9), all strikers of one target within 20 s (R10). Full scale is level L4: 15 scouts, 50 light and 35 heavy strikers.
